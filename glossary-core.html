31 Terms, In Plain English

No jargon required to understand jargon — every definition here is written for a reader who never attended the Boot Camp.

No terms match that search.

5 Whys

A technique of asking ‘why’ five times in a row on a problem to dig past the surface symptom and find its true root cause.

Acceptance Criteria (AC)

A specific, testable checklist condition that must be true for a feature to be ‘done’ -- clear enough that anyone could check pass/fail without guessing.

Accessibility (a11y)

Designing a product so people with different abilities (vision, hearing, motor, cognitive) can still use it fully -- not an optional extra feature.

Capstone

The final, cumulative project at the end of the Boot Camp where every skill learned across all 8 weeks gets applied together on one real feature.

Context (PRD section)

The background section of a document that explains the business situation and the problem being solved, so a reader with no prior history can understand why it matters.

Counter-Metric

A second number tracked alongside the North Star specifically to catch it being ‘gamed’ -- e.g. if speed goes up but accuracy secretly drops, this catches it.

Customer Journey Map

A visual timeline of every stage a customer goes through while using a product, showing where they feel confident, confused, or frustrated at each step.

Dependency

Something a feature needs from another team, system, or decision before it can ship -- each one should have a named owner and a due date.

Design Principle

A short, memorable rule a team commits to that guides every future design decision (e.g. ‘build guardrails, not cleanup workflows’).

Evidence Brief

A short document that backs up a problem statement with real data points (numbers, quotes, examples) instead of opinions.

Friction Point

A specific spot in a customer journey where something slows the customer down or causes them to hesitate, doubt, or give up.

Goals & Non-Goals

An explicit list of what a feature IS trying to do (goals) and, just as importantly, what it deliberately is NOT trying to do -- this stops scope from silently growing.

Heuristic

One of 10 well-known rules of thumb for judging whether software is easy or hard to use (e.g. ‘the system should always keep users informed’).

JTBD (Jobs To Be Done)

A way of describing what a customer is really trying to accomplish (e.g. ‘reassign a job in under 30 seconds’), instead of a demographic label.

KPI (Key Performance Indicator)

A specific, trackable number used to check whether a team is on track toward a bigger goal.

Metrics Pyramid

A way of organizing measurements into three layers: the North Star at the top, KPIs in the middle, and small day-to-day operational signals at the bottom.

NFR (Non-Functional Requirement)

A requirement about how well something must work (how fast, how secure, how reliable) rather than what it does -- e.g. a page must load in under 2 seconds.

North Star Metric

The single number a whole team rallies around, chosen because moving it up means customers are truly better off -- not just a number that looks good in a report.

Pain Point

A specific moment where a real user gets stuck, frustrated, or blocked while doing their job -- written as a verb + circumstance + consequence, not a vague complaint.

Persona

A description of a real type of user, built from actual behavior and evidence (not a guess), used to keep decisions grounded in a real person's needs.

PR/FAQ

A one-page press release plus a list of Frequently Asked Questions for a product that doesn't exist yet -- the actual document the Working Backwards method produces.

PRD (Product Requirements Document)

The official written plan for a feature: what problem it solves, what it must do, how you'll know it works, and what could go wrong -- the handoff to engineering.

Problem Statement

A short, evidence-backed paragraph naming who has the problem, what it is, and how we know it's real -- the starting line for any new feature.

RCCF

A checklist for asking an AI a genuinely useful question: Role (who should it act as), Context (the situation), Constraints (what the answer must respect), Format (how the answer should look).

Risk (in a PRD)

A specific thing that could go wrong with a feature, written down on purpose along with a plan for what to do if it happens.

Scope

The exact boundary of what is and isn't included in a piece of work.

Sign-Off

A stakeholder's explicit, named approval on a specific decision or document -- not just a passive 'no objection.'

Solution Sketch

A short, plain-language walkthrough of how a feature will work step-by-step from the user's point of view -- the visible flow, not low-level code.

Stakeholder

Any person or team who has a real interest in a decision and whose input or sign-off is needed before moving forward.

Vanity Metric

A number that goes up and looks impressive but doesn't actually prove customers are better off (e.g. total signups, when most people never come back).

Working Backwards

A planning method where you write the finished product's press release and FAQ before you build anything, so you're forced to prove real customer value first.
